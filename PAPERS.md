# Academic papers for the quantum computing game

These 36 primary papers and research manuscripts support the proposed progression, scientific boundaries, and visual metaphors in [QUANTUM-DESIGN.md](QUANTUM-DESIGN.md). They are selected sources, not an exhaustive literature survey or a hardware record leaderboard. The independent quantum reviewer checked primary landing pages, abstracts, bibliographic metadata, and relevant full-text passages; the lead analysis also checked central sources. This is a focused design review, not replication of every paper.

Publication years, preprint dates, and campaign order are different. Where useful, both preprint and journal dates are shown. A working link verifies access to a source; it does not prove a game modifier or settle every scientific limitation. The “Use and limit” column identifies what each source contributes.

## Paper cards in the finished game

The first prototype needs a focused subset of these sources. A good starting set is Q01, Q03–Q11, Q16, Q18, Q22, Q23, Q25, and Q30, with algorithm-specific sources added when their workloads unlock. The rest can appear as optional reading. Every proposed scientific project in the design table already maps to a source above.

A persistent discovery card should record the paper’s original title, authors, publication year, preprint/journal status, and direct links. Its explanation has two short parts: **What the research established** and **What this game abstracts**. Neither part should invent a validated performance multiplier. Players should be able to open the paper without buying the upgrade, and still find it after completing the discovery.

Future scenarios use explicit labels and link only to their actual inspiration. Invented researchers, imaginary papers, hardware roadmaps, and resource estimates must not be represented as demonstrated technology. An archive entry can be scientifically useful without claiming its source validates every nearby game rule.

## Papers explaining Singular Value’s scaling-law inspiration

Two additional primary sources help interpret the original game. They are not part of the 36-paper quantum catalogue.

- Jared Kaplan et al., **Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models**, 2020. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361). Empirical scaling relationships; the game’s narrative builds on this tradition.
- Jordan Hoffmann et al., **Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models**, 2022. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556). Relevant to the loss-fit coefficients and simplified tokens-per-parameter target. The game’s depth efficiency and capability conversion are additional design choices.
